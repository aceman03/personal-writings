# The Day the Gods Went Silent — Series Plan

> **SPOILERS.** Author's planning document. Not linked from the website.
> Everything here can change; the chapters are the canon.

Five books, 124 chapters, roughly 680,000 words (~2,400 printed pages).
Chapters run 15–20 pages (~4,500–6,000 words), occasionally as short as 5 or as long as 50.

## The hidden truth

Revealed slowly across all five books. Nothing here is stated outright in Book One.

**The Accord of the Sundering was a spell.** It is the only true seventh-ring circle ever
made, inscribed by the gods themselves in the **First Tongue**, the language Vaelic descends
from. It binds every god by name. That is why the gods "cannot act directly": the circle holds them.

**The Nameless One never signed the Accord, because it has no name.** It is not truly a
member of the Hollow Choir; it is older than both courts. Unbound by the circle, it spent a
thousand years learning to change it. On the day of the Silence it rewrote one ring: the
Bright Court's names now sit under *Keth* (hold) and *Nai* (stop). They are not gone.
They are bound and silenced.

**The Hollow Choir agreed to it.** The Nameless promised Karrog and Vhessane freedom from the
Accord once the Bright Court was out of the way. It lied: it means to devour them too.

**The Bright Court is fighting to get through.** Aurem can push through for seconds at a
time, only through someone who speaks the First Tongue. That is Pip's voice: *"Sorry. Can't stay."*

**The Thornwall held the Unwritten,** giant beings from before the gods' war, made of raw
world-stuff with no names of their own. Thalen poured his power into holding them for a
thousand years, and that power soaked into them. Each giant carries a sliver of the Warden.
That is why their eyes burn Thornwall blue. Freeing that sliver is the first step to freeing Thalen.

**Pip is the last Oracle.** She came out of the Wildlands from the erased city in the Empty
Basins (Tyriel's white towers and bells), whose people spoke the First Tongue and served as
the gods' voices. The Nameless One has been eating that city's memory out of the world for
centuries. Pip was carried south on a trapper's sled two winters ago by someone who knew what she was.

**The seventh ring is real.** Rewriting the Accord takes a circle of seven rings: someone with
a bottomless well to hold it (Edda), an inscriber to carve it (Brokk), spellcrafters to build
it (Ilsabet and Nyx), an Oracle to speak its center (Pip), and someone to hold the line while
they work (Aldric). Every one of them is someone the old order ranked low or not at all.

**Theme:** *A blessing is what you do with it.* Power given versus power earned, the ladders
mortals build, and whether the gods should come back on the old terms.

## The books

### Book One: The Last Dawn (22 chapters)
Survival. The Silence, Greywatch, the flight south and Fort Harrow. The party forms (Aldric,
Nyx, Pip, Hask, Brokk, Vessa). Corvel and Oren face the panicking capital; Ilsabet and Edda face
the Collegium. A giant follows Pip's gold light south. Climax at the Sunspire: Pip lights it
gold and Aurem answers for a few minutes. Reveal: the gods didn't leave, they were silenced,
and someone on the royal council knew it was coming.
**Ends:** The Nameless One speaks for the first time, to Pip.

### Book Two: What the Wall Kept (24 chapters)
The Wildlands. The party crosses the dead Thornwall going north into land unmapped for a
thousand years: the Unwritten, the skirls, the Empty Basins, the ruins of Pip's people.
They learn the giants hold slivers of Thalen. Hask's story ends here. Vessa's leash is tested.
Tyriel joins.
**Ends:** They free a sliver of Thalen, the first god partly returned, and learn the Accord is
a circle that can be rewritten. Meanwhile, the Steppe rides north.

### Book Three: The Hollow Choir (24 chapters)
War. Karrog's horde invades; Vhessane's plague spreads; the Hearthlands collapse. Corvel, the
spare prince, rises by doing what no one else can: telling who is lying. The Collegium becomes
the last stronghold. Ilsabet and Nyx begin learning the First Tongue from Pip.
**Ends:** The Nameless One betrays its allies and devours Vhessane. The Hollow Choir realizes it is next.

### Book Four: Leashless (26 chapters)
Unlikely alliances: good gods against evil gods, told through mortal go-betweens. Vessa
becomes Karrog's voice as Pip is Aurem's, and enemies are forced to work together. The search
for the original Accord circle, through Tyriel's memory and beneath the Sunspire. Heavy losses.
**Ends:** They find the circle. The Nameless One takes Pip.

### Book Five: The Seventh Ring (28 chapters)
The end. Rescuing Pip and rewriting the Accord. Edda holds all seven rings, Brokk inscribes,
Ilsabet and Nyx craft, Pip speaks the First Tongue, Aldric holds the line. The final choice:
restore the old order, with its leashes and its ladder of ranks, or write a new Accord
between gods and mortals as equals.
**Ends:** The day the gods speak again, on new terms. Epilogue: the new order of magic, where blessings
are the whole and magic is the written part of it, with spells that work with the gods' glyphs, without
them, or both (see *Future note* below).

## Future note: the new order of magic (series ending)

> Author's note, added 2026-11-10. Where the world's magic ends up by the close of Book Five.

**Today (Books One–Four), there are two separate systems:**
- **Blessings:** divine power, given by a god, drawn from nothing in the body, held on a leash.
- **Magic:** mortal power, learned, drawn from the caster's well, shaped through Vaelic circles.

They overlap only at the edges. Priests can set a god's name-glyph at the heart of a circle in place
of a Source (Chapter One), and anchored circles like the Thornwall and the healing circles run on the
god's power.

**Because magic is what saved them,** the story ends with the two systems merged. Mortal circles are
what kept people alive through the Silence: Nyx's wardstones, Edda's light, Ilsabet's rebuilt
plague-wards, and finally the seventh-ring circle that rewrites the Accord. So when the gods return,
people don't go back to simply receiving blessings. **They start writing the gods into their circles.**

**After the series, there is one system, and magic is part of it:**
- **Blessings remain the overarching system.** Divine power still exists and still comes from the gods.
- **Magic becomes a subset of it:** the written, learnable way to channel power. A circle can be drawn
  **with** a god's glyph (anchored, divine power flowing through mortal structure) or **without** one
  (mortal power from the caster's well, as now).
- **New glyphs correlate to the gods.** Beyond the old priestly name-glyphs, there is a whole vocabulary
  of god-glyphs that can sit in a circle's Source, Shape, Command or modifier rings, so a single spell can
  mix mortal and divine parts.

**What this means in practice: some spells work and some don't, whether or not the gods are present.**
- A circle anchored to a god works only while that god answers. If the god is silent, absent, bound or
  unwilling, it fails, exactly as every anchored circle failed on the first morning of the Silence.
- A mortal circle always works, gods or no gods, limited only by the caster's well and skill.
- A **hybrid** circle can fail partway: the divine rings go dark and the mortal rings keep running,
  for a weaker but surviving spell. Building in that fallback becomes the hallmark of good spellcraft
  after the Silence. Nobody wants to depend on a single thread again.
- A god's presence doesn't make every spell work, either. A badly drawn anchored circle still fails, and
  a god can choose not to answer a particular circle or caster.

**Themes this pays off:**
- *"A blessing is what you do with it."* Divine power becomes something mortals shape, not just receive.
- The old ladder (F–S ranks, Collegium classes) can't measure the new system, so it falls apart.
- The lesson of the Silence: never again build a world that runs on one thread someone else can cut.
- Moral gray carries forward. Writing the gods into circles gives mortals power over how divine power is
  used. Not everyone, mortal or divine, will think that's a good thing.

**Already seeded in the locked chapters:**
- Chapter One: priests place a god's name-glyph where a Source would go; the Thornwall is the greatest anchored circle.
- Chapter One: Ilsabet notices the Thornwall's god-anchored *Keth* rings and asks what it holds.
- Chapter Two: anchored circles fail while ordinary wardstones keep glowing (*"the gods took their own things with them"*).
- Chapter Five: Archmagus Brandt sets Ilsabet to rebuild god-anchored wards with ordinary Sources.

**Where to build it:**
- Book Two: freeing the sliver of Thalen from the giant hints that divine power can be moved through a circle.
- Book Three: Ilsabet and Nyx learn the First Tongue, the language the gods themselves wrote circles in.
- Book Four: Vessa and Pip act as voices for Karrog and Aurem; the first deliberate hybrid circles.
- Book Five: the new Accord is itself the first true hybrid circle, and the epilogue shows the new order in use.

## Character arcs across the series

| Character | Arc |
|---|---|
| **Sir Aldric Vane** | From a man whose power was given to one who earns it. Learns to fight, lead and trust mages without a god. In Book Five he is offered his blessing back on the old terms and must decide whether to take it. |
| **Nyx Thornbriar** | Self-taught hedge-witch to great spellcrafter. Bloodcasts successfully once (Book One) and carries glyph-shaped scars. Learns the First Tongue. Her bargain with Aldric becomes the series' central partnership. |
| **Pip** | The last Oracle. Learns who she is and where she came from. The voice that is "sorry" is Aurem. Taken by the Nameless One in Book Four. |
| **Sergeant Hask Morrow** | The steady everyman and the party's practical survivor. Dies in Book Two, beyond the wall he guarded for twenty-two years. |
| **Brokk Ironmantle** | Dwarven forge-master who brings word that something is waking under the mountains. Inscription is his craft, and inscription is how the new Accord will be carved. |
| **Vessa** | Orc champion of Karrog whose blessing still works. Breaks from Urzag's warband in Book One. Her leash is the series' moral test; in Book Four she becomes Karrog's voice on her own terms. |
| **Prince Corvel** | The spare prince graded E for truth-sense. Loses the blessing at the Silence, and discovers that twelve years of it taught him to read people without it. Exposes the council traitor in Book One; becomes a leader in Book Three by telling who is lying, with no god's help at all. |
| **Father Oren Vasse** | Corvel's protector and conscience. Holds on to faith in a silent god. |
| **Edda Marsh** | The fisherman's daughter with a bottomless well. Grows from first-year student to the one person who can hold a seventh ring. |
| **Ilsabet Corrow** | The spellcrafter who asked what the Thornwall was holding. Unlocked from her tower; becomes the party's scholar. |
| **Magus Teodric Hale** | Inherited-magic traditionalist who has to admit Ilsabet was right. |
| **Tyriel** | Elven memory-keeper who discovers that Ithrel's oldest memories are being erased. Joins in Book Two. |
| **Urzag** | Karrog's champion and the Steppe's war leader. Primary mortal antagonist of Books One and Three. |
| **Sereth** | Eldest Dawnwarden. Catches Aurem's light one final time at the Sunspire. |
| **The Nameless One** | The true antagonist. Speaks first at the end of Book One. |
